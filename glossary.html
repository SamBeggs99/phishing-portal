Glossary

Plain-English definitions for security terms used throughout this training portal. No technical background required.

A B C D E M O P R S T V Z
A
Authentication
Access Control
The process of verifying that you are who you say you are. Entering your username and password is authentication. Multi-factor authentication adds a second verification step on top.
Attachment (malicious)
Phishing
A file sent via email that, when opened, installs malware or runs harmful code on your computer. Common formats used in attacks include .pdf, .docx, .zip, and .exe files that appear legitimate.
Example: An invoice PDF that, when opened, silently installs software that records your keystrokes.
B
BEC — Business Email Compromise
Attack Type
An attack where criminals impersonate a company executive or trusted vendor via email to trick employees into transferring money, sharing credentials, or taking other harmful actions. No malware is needed — it relies entirely on deception.
Example: An email appearing to be from the CFO asking for urgent gift card purchases.
Barracuda
PAC Tool
The email security platform PAC uses to filter spam, detect phishing, and allow employees to report suspicious emails. The Barracuda button in your Outlook ribbon lets you report a suspicious email directly to IT Security with one click.
C
Credential Stuffing
Attack Technique
An automated attack where criminals take username/password combinations leaked in one data breach and automatically try them on hundreds of other websites. If you reuse passwords, one breach on any site gives attackers access to everything else.
Credentials
Access Control
Your login information — typically a username (or email address) and a password. Credentials are the primary target of most phishing attacks because they provide direct access to your accounts.
D
Data Breach
Security Incident
An incident where confidential information is accessed, stolen, or disclosed without authorization. Breaches can affect employee records, client data, financial information, and intellectual property. Most start with a phishing attack or stolen credentials.
Dwell Time
Incident Response
The time between an attacker gaining access to a network and being detected. The global average is over 200 days. Long dwell time means more damage, more data stolen, and more expensive remediation. Rapid incident reporting reduces dwell time.
E
Encryption
Data Protection
The process of scrambling data so that only authorized parties can read it. When you use the PAC VPN, your internet traffic is encrypted — meaning anyone who intercepts it sees only unreadable scrambled data instead of your actual files or communications.
External Email Flag
PAC Tool
A warning label automatically added to emails that come from outside the PAC organization. If you see [External] at the top of an email, it means the sender's address is not a @pacgroup.com address. This is a key signal to be more cautious before clicking or acting.
M
Malware
Attack Type
Malicious software — any program designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to a computer system. Malware includes viruses, ransomware, spyware, and keyloggers. It is most commonly delivered via phishing emails or malicious downloads.
MFA — Multi-Factor Authentication
Access Control
A security method that requires two or more forms of verification before granting access to an account. Typically: something you know (your password) plus something you have (your phone, an authenticator app, or a hardware key). MFA stops most credential-based attacks even when a password is stolen.
Example: After entering your password, you get a notification on your phone that you must approve before access is granted.
MitM — Man-in-the-Middle Attack
Attack Technique
An attack where a criminal secretly intercepts and potentially alters communications between two parties who believe they are communicating directly with each other. Common on public Wi-Fi networks. A VPN prevents MitM attacks by encrypting all your traffic.
O
OSINT — Open Source Intelligence
Attack Technique
The practice of gathering information about a target using only publicly available sources — social media, LinkedIn, company websites, news articles, and public records. Attackers use OSINT to research employees before crafting targeted spear phishing attacks.
P
Password Manager
Security Tool
A software application that securely stores and generates complex, unique passwords for every account. You only need to remember one master password. Password managers eliminate the need to reuse passwords and are the single most practical way to improve password security.
Phishing
Attack Type
A cyberattack that uses deceptive emails, text messages, or phone calls to trick people into revealing credentials, clicking malicious links, or taking harmful actions. Phishing relies on psychological manipulation — urgency, fear, authority, and scarcity — rather than technical hacking.
Q
Quishing — QR Code Phishing
Attack Type
A phishing attack delivered via QR code. The QR code is embedded in an email, flyer, or physical sign. When scanned, it takes the user to a malicious website. Most people cannot verify where a QR code leads before scanning it, which makes this an increasingly common attack method.
R
Ransomware
Attack Type
A type of malware that encrypts the victim's files or entire computer system and demands a payment (ransom) to restore access. Most ransomware attacks start with a phishing email. Even when a ransom is paid, data is not always restored. Ransomware is one of the most costly threats facing organizations globally.
S
Shadow IT
Policy
Technology used within an organization that is not approved, managed, or known by IT. Examples include personal Dropbox accounts used for work files, unapproved apps installed on work laptops, or personal messaging apps used for business communication. Shadow IT creates security risks because it bypasses organizational controls.
Smishing — SMS Phishing
Attack Type
Phishing attacks delivered via text message (SMS). Attackers send texts impersonating banks, delivery companies, or IT support to trick recipients into clicking malicious links or sharing credentials. Because text messages feel more personal than emails, smishing can be harder to recognize.
Social Engineering
Attack Technique
The use of psychological manipulation to trick people into making security mistakes or giving away sensitive information. Social engineering exploits human tendencies — trust, helpfulness, fear, urgency — rather than technical vulnerabilities. Phishing is one form of social engineering.
Spear Phishing
Attack Type
A targeted phishing attack that uses personal information about the victim — their name, role, colleagues, or current projects — to appear more credible. Unlike generic phishing (sent to thousands of people), spear phishing is crafted for a specific individual and is significantly harder to detect.
Spoofing
Attack Technique
Faking the identity of a sender, website, or phone number to appear legitimate. Email spoofing makes a message appear to come from a trusted address. Caller ID spoofing makes a fraudulent call appear to come from a known number. Always verify identity through a separate, trusted channel.
T
Tailgating
Physical Security
A physical security attack where an unauthorized person gains access to a restricted area by following closely behind an authorized person through a secured door, without badging in. Tailgating exploits social politeness — the natural instinct to hold doors open for others.
Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)
Access Control
A form of multi-factor authentication (MFA) that requires exactly two forms of verification. The most common combination is a password plus a one-time code sent to your phone or generated by an authenticator app. 2FA and MFA are often used interchangeably.
V
Vishing — Voice Phishing
Attack Type
Phishing conducted over the phone. Attackers call impersonating IT support, banks, executives, or government agencies to extract credentials, authorize fraudulent transactions, or install remote access software. The caller often knows personal details about the target to appear credible.
VPN — Virtual Private Network
Security Tool
A technology that creates an encrypted tunnel between your device and a secure server, protecting your internet traffic from interception. PAC provides a VPN that must be used whenever accessing company systems outside the office. It is especially critical on public Wi-Fi networks.
Z
Zero-Day
Vulnerability
A software vulnerability that is unknown to the vendor and has no available patch or fix. Zero-day attacks exploit these unknown weaknesses before developers have had a chance to address them. Keeping software updated reduces (but cannot eliminate) zero-day risk.
